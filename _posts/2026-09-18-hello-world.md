Somehow, "blogging is dead" has been the consensus for about fifteen years now — and yet here I am, hitting publish on a brand-new personal tech blog in 2026. Let me explain myself.

## The short version

I learn things, I forget things, and then I re-learn the same things six months later. This blog is my attempt to break that cycle. Every post here is a note to future-me, published in public just in case it helps present-you too.

> The best way to learn something deeply is to explain it to someone else. The second best way is to write it down where your future self can find it.
{: .callout}

## What you'll find here

This isn't a niche blog with a content strategy and a posting calendar. It's a personal one, so expect whatever I'm genuinely interested in at the time. In practice, that means:

- **Practical tutorials** — things I actually built, with working code you can copy.
- **Deep dives** — topics like closures, CSS layout, or Git internals, explained the way I wish someone had explained them to me.
- **Field notes** — tools, setups and workflows I'm trying out, with honest verdicts.
- **Opinionated rants (occasionally)** — I promise to keep these short and well-argued.

## What you won't find here

- AI-generated filler. Every word here was typed by a human (me), fueled by coffee.
- "10x developer" hustle content. I write code, I make mistakes, I fix them. That's the whole brand.
- Paywalls, popups, or newsletter guilt-trips. There's an [RSS feed]({{ '/feed.xml' | relative_url }}) and that's it.

## Why a blog and not just tweets or videos?

Nothing against short-form content, but some ideas need more than 280 characters and a thread emoji. A blog post:

1. **Is searchable** — Google can find it years later. Good luck finding a tweet from 2023.
2. **Is permanent** — I own this site. No algorithm decides who sees it.
3. **Forces clarity** — writing 1,000 words about something exposes every gap in my understanding.

That last point is the real reason I'm here. Writing is thinking, and publishing is accountability.

## The plan

New posts every couple of weeks. If you want to follow along, grab the [RSS feed]({{ '/feed.xml' | relative_url }}), star the [repo on GitHub](https://github.com/mrprecioustech/mrprecioustech.github.io), or just bookmark the site like it's 2009.

And if you're thinking of starting your own blog: do it. It costs nothing — I built this entire site with Jekyll and GitHub Pages for free, and I wrote a [whole tutorial about it]({% link _posts/2026-09-10-jekyll-github-pages-blog.md %}).

See you in the next post. ✍
